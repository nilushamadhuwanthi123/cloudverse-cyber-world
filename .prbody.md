Phase 4 of the master prompt: Cyber navigation and information architecture.

The Cyber District was one long page of panels with nowhere to grow. It is now a rail of nine named sectors and a stage.

## Three states, not two

| State | Means | Shown as |
|---|---|---|
| `online` | Built and open right now | `ONLINE` |
| `locked` | Built, not earned yet | `LOCKED` |
| `planned` | Named here, not built yet | `OFFLINE` |

Most navigation collapses the last two into "locked". That is a lie — it tells the player to keep playing for something no amount of play will deliver. Cyber Arcade is not something you unlock in this build; it is something that does not exist yet, and the rail says so. The header states the split out loud ("3 of 9 sectors online · 0 locked · 6 not deployed yet"), because a rail of nine where six are unavailable reads as a broken build until it explains itself.

## What ships

- **SEC-01 Cyber Operations** — threat monitor, defense rules, severity, missions
- **SEC-02 Incident Response** — gated behind the `first-response` mission, so the unlock system is visible without holding anything back
- **SEC-03 Security Analytics** — moved *inside* Cyber, where it belongs. As a fifth top-level tab it sat at the same level as the districts it analyses.
- **SEC-04…09** — Digital Forensics, Network Operations, Security Intelligence, Security Lab, Cyber Academy, Cyber Arcade: named, described, honestly marked offline

## Architecture

What exists is content (`data/cyberSectors.js`). What is reachable right now is a function of live progress (`game/cyberNavigation.js`). Keeping those apart means the rail's states are assertable as plain values rather than inspected in a rendered DOM:

```js
resolveSectors({ completedMissionIds: [] })
  .find((s) => s.id === 'incident-response').state // 'locked'
```

`CyberDistrict` decides layout and nothing else about navigation.

## Two things the browser caught

**Every click on a locked row looked like a dead control.** `landingSector` fell back to Operations for anything not online, so selecting a locked sector bounced straight back. But selecting a locked sector is *how the player learns what opens it*. It now keeps whatever sector was asked for as long as this build has it, and falls back only for an id that does not exist at all — which still protects against a save file naming a renamed or removed sector.

**`aria-disabled` was a lie.** It promises a control that does nothing; these do something useful. It is gone. State reaches assistive technology through the `ONLINE` / `LOCKED` / `OFFLINE` word inside the tab, which is part of its accessible name and is never carried by colour alone.

## Keyboard

A real `tablist`: arrows move and wrap, `Home`/`End` jump to the ends, roving tabindex keeps `Tab` moving to the content rather than through nine items, and the panel is `aria-labelledby` its tab with a visible focus ring.

## Entry sequence

Four lines — secure channel, operations online, threat monitor, defense sync — then the interface. Theatre that blocks the interface has to be honest about it: **Skip** is focused first, `prefers-reduced-motion` skips it outright rather than playing it faster, the lines share one polite live region, and every timer is cleared on unmount.
